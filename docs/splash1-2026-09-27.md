## Splash starfield coverage correction (2026-10-25)

The ordinary splash previously placed 750 equal-color, distance-attenuated
points in a finite world-Z box. Angled views sampled that box unevenly and
could look beyond its edges, leaving the left sky sparse or empty. This was
independent of the black-hole prototype's separate procedural background.

The ordinary splash now uses 6,000 deterministic, seeded random directions on
a radius-300 sphere, within the existing camera far plane and well outside the
foreground/body composition. Uniform probability per solid angle removes a
preferred world axis without forcing evenly spaced stars: natural gaps and
small groupings remain. Seeded brightness variation favors dimmer stars.
Star colors reuse the existing B/A/F/G/K/M representative spectral palette,
softened toward white: blue-white, white, pale yellow and warm orange/red.
This is an authored visible-star mix, not a measured spectral population.
Color selection is hashed independently of placement/brightness, preserving
their seeded sequence and the debris arrangement when changing the palette.
Points use a small 1.25 CSS-pixel size before device-pixel scaling rather than
distance attenuation; normal depth testing preserves body occlusion and the
star material does not write depth. This is an artistic sky, not HYG positions
or a reconstruction of Milky Way structure. No catalog data or source imagery
is used. The random startup pool and black-hole shader sky are unchanged.

Both existing foreground choices use this correction. Their procedural debris
remains deterministic, although its exact arrangement changes because it shares
the backdrop's seeded random sequence. No debris count, clearance policy,
body placement or camera behavior changes.

Validation includes geometric per-quadrant coverage at portrait/wide aspects,
both initial camera directions and orbit directions, reproducibility, brightness
variation and resource disposal. A real WebGL pixel test isolates the starfield
from foregrounds and UI, samples the actual splash viewport, and checks that
all four quadrants retain stars before/after orbit and resize for mining/station
views. Bounds allow random variation; they do not require an evenly spaced sky.

### Scientific references versus presentation

- [Miller-Jones et al. (2021), Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09091):
  black-hole mass 21.2 +/- 2.2 solar masses; companion mass 40.6 solar masses;
  representative companion radius 22.3 solar radii; temperature 31,138 K;
  relative orbital semimajor axis 0.244 AU (reported bounds 0.231-0.256 AU);
  eccentricity 0.0189; inclination 27.51 degrees; distance about 2.22 kpc.
  The 0.160 AU value in that paper is the black hole's orbit about the
  barycenter, **not** the separation between the two bodies.
- [Jiang (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12507): the approximately 5.6-day
  binary is fed by its supergiant companion's stellar wind.
- [Astronexus HYG v4.4](https://codeberg.org/astronexus/hyg), CC BY-SA 4.0:
  HYG 97987 / HIP 98298 / HD 226868 is the visible companion/system anchor
  labeled Cygnus X-1, not a separate black-hole entity. Its older distance,
  luminosity and B0Ib spectral entry are not used as current physical values.
  These sources are attributed on the public intro page in English and Italian;
  the two scientific papers are also linked in the prototype controls.

The [source profile](../src/app/model/black-hole/cygnus-x1.ts) keeps the physical
values separate from authored splash parameters. The nominal Schwarzschild
radius is approximately 62.6 km, versus about 36.5 million km between the
body centers. A companion visible next to a detailed shadow cannot be read
as an equal-scale system diagram. This prototype uses an authored angular
companion and disk extent, not a physical companion orbit or binary simulation.
Disk hue, brightness, turbulence and time scale are illustrative. The shader's
blue-white companion reuses the procedural star renderer as an authored
off-axis portrait, not a measured surface image or a star at infinity behind
the lens. It is not passed through the black-hole lens or placed using the
physical binary separation. This avoids inventing a behind-the-hole alignment
and Einstein arcs in the approved composition. No claim is made that this is
the naked-eye appearance.

### Shared core and accuracy boundary

The Angular-independent [render handle](../src/app/scene/black-hole/black-hole.ts)
owns a bounded-resolution offscreen pass and a camera-facing display plane.
The splash adapter supplies camera-frame mapping and quality budgets. A later
gameplay adapter can reuse that ownership/rendering pattern, but must supply
real scale-aware placement and may require a different rendering strategy:
there is deliberately no pretend scientific-viewer mode in this milestone.

The lens integrator uses the Schwarzschild null-geodesic orbit equation in
Schwarzschild-radius units, with adaptive velocity-Verlet integration. It
captures rays at the horizon, samples a thin disk at ray/plane crossings,
and bends the procedural background stars along escaped rays.
A [CPU reference](../src/app/model/black-hole/schwarzschild.ts) checks the
analytical critical impact parameter, weak-field bending and step convergence.
Disk shading adds illustrative orbital turbulence, approximate circular-orbit
Doppler beaming and gravitational redshift. This is **not Kerr ray tracing**:
Cygnus X-1's high spin, frame dragging, spin-dependent ISCO, full radiative
transfer, spectral response and physical disk/wind structure are not modeled.
The inner disk edge at 3 Schwarzschild radii is the non-rotating ISCO, not
a claim about Cygnus X-1's actual inner disk radius.

Low uses a 320 x 267 RGBA16F target and 256 integration steps; standard uses
640 x 533 and 384 steps. The target alone is approximately 0.65 / 2.60 MiB,
without depth, stencil or mipmaps. `EXT_color_buffer_float` is required;
unsupported hardware reports an explicit splash error with existing still-image
and retry controls. The existing mining/station poster remains the fallback,
not black-hole-specific imagery. The main canvas resolution is unchanged.
Reduced motion freezes disk animation; unchanged camera/time reuses the
previous ray image. Hidden scenes stop updates. Disposal removes the display
and releases both pass/display geometries, materials and the target.

## 3. Why the original must not be the production splash asset

At a sustained 10 Mbps, the original alone requires approximately **141 seconds** to transfer; at 50 Mbps, approximately **28 seconds**. These are idealized transfer calculations before latency, contention, decoding, shader compilation, and GPU upload.

Geometry alone represents about 139.79 MiB of GPU buffer payload. Assuming RGBA8 texture allocation and full mip chains, the three textures would consume approximately **192 MiB**. Together that is roughly **332 MiB** before framebuffers, shadows, other scene objects, decoder overhead, or CPU-side copies. Actual allocation is renderer/device dependent and must be measured.

Reducing only texture size would leave roughly 140 MiB of geometry. Geometry compression reduces transfer size but does not, by itself, reduce triangle count or the decoded geometry workload.

There is also a delivery issue:

- [angular.json](../angular.json) copies everything under `public` into build assets. Merely avoiding a runtime reference does not remove the source from deployment.
- Angular's initial JavaScript bundle budget is not a budget for an independently fetched GLB.
- The original exceeds GitHub's ordinary 100 MiB per-file limit. Do not commit it as a regular Git blob.

Keep the original unchanged while planning. Before implementation ships, agree on source storage outside deployed assets, or a precise asset-copy exclusion. If versioning the original is desired, agree on Git LFS or other approved storage and its hosting implications first. Never upload it to an external optimizer without approval.

## 5. Asset preparation pipeline

1. **Establish a visual baseline.** Open the source locally in Blender or a compatible glTF viewer; record its best angle, material appearance, and silhouette. Confirm the usable front orientation and whether back faces are actually required.
2. **Create derivative copies.** Keep the supplied files intact. Start from the new 154k and 50k Meshy remeshes rather than repeating simplification. Further local simplification in Blender is contingent on visual/performance results. Preserve UV seams, normals, thin structural members, and the hero silhouette.
3. **Bake where needed.** If remeshing changes topology/UVs, bake source detail onto the new mesh. Do not assume simple decimation preserves texture quality. Inspect cranes, railings, cavities, and panels at the intended screen size.
4. **Prepare two quality tiers.** The supplied remeshes already meet the 150,000-250,000 standard and 40,000-80,000 low-quality hero triangle targets. Reduce their maps to at most 2K for standard and 1K for low quality. These are starting targets, not guarantees of acceptable appearance.
5. **Correct materials.** Preserve glTF color-space semantics: base color/emissive use sRGB; normal/metallic/roughness remain linear data. Review the JPEG normal map for artifacts. Author selective emissive detail rather than making the entire base-color map glow.
6. **Optimize delivery.** Evaluate local glTF Transform tooling for pruning, deduplication, geometry quantization/compression, and KTX2/Basis texture conversion. Prefer one geometry codec, selected after comparing size, decode cost, and visual results; do not stack alternatives unnecessarily.
7. **Wire decoder support before enabling compressed assets.** The existing `GLTFLoader` usage does not establish Draco, Meshopt, or KTX2 support. Configure the selected decoder/transcoder, self-host its runtime files, verify deployment paths/CSP, and include their bytes and startup cost in budgets.
8. **Produce a poster.** Render the approved composition into a responsive, compressed image. Use this immediately while 3D loads and when 3D is unavailable. The direction image is not automatically the final poster.
9. **Validate and record.** Run a glTF validator, check dimensions/counts/size, compare renders with the original, and record the source fingerprint, tool versions, exact processing settings, and output hashes.

Suggested derivative names: `asteroid-mining-rig.standard.glb`, `asteroid-mining-rig.low.glb`, and `mining-splash-poster.webp`. Version or hash runtime asset URLs explicitly; do not assume copied public assets receive bundle filename hashing.

Do not fetch standard and low variants simultaneously or load both just to choose one. Start with a conservative selected tier. Add upgrades only if profiling justifies the additional transfer and transient memory.

### Implementation structure and lifecycle

- Implement a standalone Angular Three splash scene using the existing shared canvas and signal/computed conventions. Keep composition and asset loading local until actual reuse warrants extraction.
- Add a small typed splash state only where needed to bridge the scene and its DOM poster/status overlay: idle, loading, ready, error, and intentionally static.
- Keep the poster/status layer outside the Three.js scene graph. Keep login, registration, and navigation usable independently of asset readiness.
- Load only optimized local assets when the splash is relevant. Initially preserve the five-second intro transition; show the poster during subsequent loading. Evaluate idle prefetch only after measuring contention with the initial UI.
- Transition off the poster only after the selected model, textures, and required environment resources can render a complete frame, not merely after the HTTP request finishes.
- On asset/decoder errors, log through the existing `appLogger`, retain the poster, show a localized unobtrusive status, and provide an explicit retry or static-view action. Do not silently claim the 3D scene loaded.
- Handle unsupported WebGL/context loss at the canvas host so the fallback works even when the scene component cannot initialize.
- Respect reduced motion, hidden documents, and actual scene occlusion. Demand mode is useful only if splash animation callbacks are not continually invalidating hidden/static frames.
- Cancel owned requests where supported and guard late async completion after navigation. Release event listeners, animation hooks, geometry, textures, materials, and owned image resources. If assets are cached, define ownership so unmounting one consumer cannot dispose another consumer's resources.
- Restore camera, background, environment, exposure, and renderer settings changed by the splash. Avoid global renderer-quality changes that affect game scenes.
- Add new user-visible strings to both [en.ts](../src/app/i18n/locales/en.ts) and [it.ts](../src/app/i18n/locales/it.ts).

No backend or socket-contract changes are needed for a decorative splash.

Frame-time and transfer budgets must be met together. A tiny compressed download can still be expensive to decode/render. Low-tier selection should use supported capability/preference signals conservatively, not user-agent strings alone; preserve a static fallback and avoid quality-tier oscillation.

### Measurement protocol

- Record hardware, OS, browser, graphics backend, viewport, panel split, DPR, quality tier, asset hashes, and cold/warm cache state.
- Test desktop at 1440 x 900 with the default split and a representative mobile device at 390 x 844. Include narrow/wide panel resizing.
- Use a repeatable cold-cache network profile of 10 Mbps download and 100 ms RTT. Record CPU throttling separately; a desktop browser under throttling does not replace real mobile testing.
- Target usable public controls and poster within 2.5 seconds of navigation under the chosen profile. Record full-page LCP separately from poster timing; preserve or improve baseline if existing application startup is slower.
- Under that network profile, target the complete standard scene within 12 seconds of the asset-load trigger, low tier within 6 seconds. The existing five-second intro delay is additional and must be reported separately.
- After a five-second warmup, capture at least 30 seconds of animation and report p50/p95 frame intervals, long frames, draw calls, triangles, and effects/shadow passes.
- Use browser network/performance traces for bytes, decoding, upload stalls, and responsiveness. Use `renderer.info` for counts, not as a GPU byte meter; estimate resource bytes from formats/dimensions and corroborate where tooling permits.
- Repeat enter/leave cycles ten times. Verify owned resource counts and listeners return to baseline, or to a documented bounded cache plateau, with no monotonic memory growth.
- Verify the original 176.52 MB source is neither requested nor present in the production artifact.

## Cygnus X-1 rendering reuse in Stellar Viewer (2026-10-25)

The shared `scene/black-hole/black-hole.ts` geodesic pass is also consumed by the
Stellar Viewer. The Viewer supplies only the black-hole ray image; it does not
reuse the splash portrait composition or overlay the splash companion. Selection
is the exact `catalogIdentity` pair `cygnus-x1/black-hole` together with the
contract-compatible `black-hole` body type and surface archetype. The companion
is separately identified by `cygnus-x1/hd226868`; names, HYG IDs, entity IDs and
texture hints are not aliases.

The renderer owns a bounded 320 × 267 HDR target, restores renderer state after
its offscreen pass, freezes its animation for reduced-motion users and disposes
its target/materials/geometries with the Angular component lifecycle. Devices
without `EXT_color_buffer_float` receive a logged and user-visible fallback
notice. The ray tracer is Schwarzschild/ non-rotating, while Cygnus X-1 rotates.
The displayed black-hole/companion spacing is explicitly a presentation layout:
both canonical positions remain authored zero-origin barycentric snapshots, the
system distance and position remain unknown, and no orbit, phase or orientation
is inferred. The source Schwarzschild radius is a non-rotating reference;
unknown event-horizon and visual-disk radii remain null.

The Viewer list exposes a local Cygnus X-1 catalog entry without widening its
distance-limited system-list request. Selecting it requests the registered
player's `solar-system-get`; the API remains responsible for seeding the system.
When the companion appears in both response collections, Viewer and details
consume a single row per entity ID.
